# v0.5.2 — The Evolution of Civilization

Five hundred years of history: the village grows through five 100-year ages, from timber cottages to
skyscrapers and solar spires, because its people decide to build and can pay for it. Nothing is a
cut-scene. An age only makes things possible, and every coin still moves through the ledger.

## Highlights

- **Five ages.** Age of Foundations (Year 1), Industrial Awakening (101), Modern Expansion (201),
  Metropolitan Age (301) and Age of Tomorrow (401), on the existing calendar.
- **25 technologies.** Each has its own prerequisites and local needs. The council adopts and pays for them one
  at a time, and new architecture waits for its technology.
- **Utilities.** Power, water, communications and transit are laid district by district as paid public works, with
  upkeep. Buildings beyond their district's service run at reduced capacity.
- **Procedural architecture for every era.** Each building type has a form for every era. New apartment blocks,
  residential towers, mansions, railway stations and hospitals. Zoning keeps towers in the centre.
- **Real modernization.** Owners decide whether to rebuild, and a rebuild means demolition and then construction,
  with paid builders and materials bought locally. Developers acquire land legally and let flats; wealthy
  families commission mansions.
- **Heritage and preservation.** Buildings gain historic importance. Campaigns, planning votes, the Heritage
  Preservation Act, listed buildings, conservation areas and court injunctions protect them.
- **Then & Now.** Every property keeps a permanent record of its changes. Pick any year to see that year's
  building in 3D beside today's, with a slider between them and the chain of owners.
- **Transport evolution.** Cars, buses, rapid transit and autonomous pods; freight by rail, lorry and automated
  cargo; roads resurfaced to asphalt and later smart roads.
- **Original sound and music.** Everything is synthesised live with Web Audio, with no recordings and no
  borrowed music:
  - at least two pieces per age, plus themes for festivals, winter, New Year, war and disasters;
  - an adaptive music director;
  - ambience based on what is around you and its age;
  - positional effects;
  - a five-bus mixer.
  Nothing plays until you tap *Enable sound*.
- **Installable app.** An original app icon, a web app manifest and a title screen. A service worker tied to
  each build lets the game start offline. To install on an iPhone, open it in Safari, then Share → *Add to
  Home Screen*.
- **Mobile performance.** Building geometry is merged, tall buildings get distant stand-ins, street lights are
  batched, rebuilds are spread over several frames, resolution adapts when frames are slow, and a
  battery-saver mode is available.

## Saves

Save format 8. Older kingdoms upgrade on load without changing coins, goods, people or dates. **The original
save is kept on the device first** and can be downloaded from Council → *Pre-upgrade copy*. The cloud service
refuses to let an older game version overwrite a kingdom saved in the newer format.

## Testing

- **Unit suites:** 174 node:test suites.
- **Touch end-to-end test:** Chromium emulating an iPhone 13.
- **Tiered longevity tests:**
  - `test:longevity:quick`: 25 years × 4 seeds, about 20 s.
  - `test:longevity:century`: 100 years × 4 seeds, about 3.5 min; runs on every push.
  - `test:longevity:full`: 500 years × 4 seeds; the release check. It saves age-start checkpoints that
    `test:longevity:ages` can resume.
- **Simulation speed:** the busiest parts of the simulation are 10–15% faster. For the same seed the final
  world is byte-identical, which `npm run bench` checks.

## Time to reach Year 500 in play

Year 500 is 862,224 ticks of 0.25 s at 1×. Time only advances while the game is open, unpaused and in the
foreground.

| Speed | Real time |
|---|---|
| 1× | ~59.9 hours |
| 3× | ~20.0 hours |
| 6× | ~10.0 hours |
| 20× | ~3.0 hours |

## Known limitations

- Not tested on a physical iPhone. The app install, audio unlock, orientation, offline start and touch controls
  were checked in Chromium emulating an iPhone 13.
- Frame rates were measured only under software rendering.
- The population stays at village-to-town scale, about 80–160 people, each simulated individually.
- The player cannot list a building directly.
- The optional cloud backup is implemented but not deployed.
